## HOW TO USE THIS FILE

**Where it lives:** `C:\Users\KATANA\Desktop\my website\loopgem\social-content-bank.md`
Open it any time. No need to look in chat.

**Your routine (about 5 min, twice a week):**
1. Open this file.
2. Start at the TOP and find the first post with an empty `- [ ]` box.
3. Copy it into Buffer. Select **both X and Threads** so one entry posts to both.
4. Come back here and change that post's `- [ ]` to `- [x]`.
5. Repeat until Buffer's queue is full (free plan holds about 10 per channel).
6. Save the file.

**Rule of thumb: empty box `[ ]` = not posted yet. Checked box `[x]` = already used, skip it.**
Always work top to bottom so you never lose your place.

**Important:** edit this file **on your computer**, not on GitHub.com. The weekly agent pushes new batches from the local copy, and editing on the website puts them out of sync.

**New posts arrive automatically every Sunday**, added at the BOTTOM under a dated heading like `### Batch 2026-08-02`. So the unposted ones naturally collect toward the end of the file as you work down.

**Post rules**
- Every post stays **under 280 characters** so it works on X and reads fast on Threads.
- **Cadence: 3-4 posts per day**, each posted to both platforms.
- Roughly **1 in 4** posts carries a CTA (link or "DM me"). The rest build trust.

### Story and proof
- [ ] I sold my first beat for $25 in 2019. It took me 4 months to get that one sale. Since then I've delivered 2,019 orders. What changed: I stopped guessing and learned how buyers search and what makes them click order.

- [ ] 8 years on Fiverr. 982 clients. About a third of my income came from repeat buyers. Getting a client once is marketing. Keeping them is the business.

- [ ] Started in a bedroom in Casablanca with zero followers. $127k later, the thing that moved the needle was learning to sell, not learning another plugin.

- [ ] Nobody bought my beats for 4 months. The same beats got orders once I rewrote my gig title and fixed my pricing tiers. The music was fine the whole time.

- [ ] 982 clients later the pattern is clear. Buyers don't want beats. They want a finished song they're proud to send to their friends. Write your listing about that and watch your conversion rate move.

### Pricing
- [ ] Most producers price at $5 to compete. Then they get clients who argue over every revision. Cheap pricing filters for difficult buyers. Raise your floor and your client quality changes overnight.

- [ ] Your beat is not worth $20 because it took you an hour. It's worth what the outcome is worth to the artist. Price the result, not your time.

- [ ] Three tiers beats one price every time. Basic gets them in the door. Standard is where most land. Premium makes Standard look reasonable. Most producers offer one and leave money behind.

- [ ] If you sell on Fiverr they take 20 percent. Your $50 order pays you $40. Price with the fee in mind or you'll hit your goal on paper and miss it in your bank account.

- [ ] What to charge if you're new: basic lease $20 to $30. WAV and stems $40 to $60. Exclusive or custom $150 and up. Never open at $5. That's a trap you'll spend a year climbing out of.

### Services over beats
- [ ] Mixing converts easier than beats. The buyer already has a song, already knows what they need, already has budget. Beats ask them to imagine something. Sell what people already know they want.

- [ ] My order volume tripled the month I added mixing next to beats. Same skills, same DAW, different offer. Most producers are one service away from real income.

- [ ] "Beat" is a product. "I'll make your song sound finished" is an outcome. One earns $30. The other earns $150. Same work.

### Tactical
- [ ] Fiverr buyer requests are the fastest path to your first order. Buyers post what they need. A specific five line reply beats a huge portfolio when you have zero reviews.

- [ ] Your first order matters for the review, not the money. One 5 star review turns a dead storefront into one buyers trust. Over deliver on the first three and the rest compound.

- [ ] Put your 3 best samples on your gig. Not 20. Buyers judge you by your weakest track, so stop showing them your whole hard drive.

- [ ] The producers getting orders aren't posting more. They're replying to more buyers. Ten thoughtful DMs will out convert a hundred posts into the void.

- [ ] Ask for the review. Most producers deliver the files and go quiet. One polite message after delivery is the difference between 4 reviews and 40.

- [ ] Gig getting views but no orders? Check three things before you touch your music. Your first sample. Your pricing. Your title. I fixed those three and went from zero to steady orders.

- [ ] Send the files, tell them what you did, tell them how to ask for changes, then ask for the review. That one habit took me from a handful of reviews to hundreds.

### Mindset and hot takes
- [ ] You don't need 10k followers to sell beats. You need a few hundred people who make music and an offer that converts them. I've seen 400 real followers out earn 40k bought ones.

- [ ] Buying followers kills your engagement rate, so the algorithm shows your posts to fewer real humans. You're paying to be seen less.

- [ ] "I'll start selling once my beats are better" is how producers stay broke for 3 years. Your beats are good enough. Your offer is the problem.

- [ ] Talent is not the bottleneck. I've heard bedroom producers outproduce people charging $500. The gap is that one of them knows how to get in front of buyers.

- [ ] Consistency is not posting every day. Consistency is following up with the client who went quiet and asking the buyer from March if they need more work.

- [ ] Sitting on 200 beats and no sales? You don't have a music problem. You have a distribution problem. Pick one place buyers already are and set up one strong offer.

- [ ] The most talented producer I know still works retail. He refuses to track his numbers, follow up, or ask for reviews. Skill gets you repeat clients. Selling gets you the first one.
